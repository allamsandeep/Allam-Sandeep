SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle):
SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle) is a step-by-step process used to design, develop, test, and maintain software in a systematic way.  
  
SDLC Models:
* Waterfall Model:
  Linear and sequential
  Each phase completes before next starts
  Advantages: Simple and easy to understand , Good for small projects
  Disadvantages: Not flexible , Changes are difficult
  Use case: Small projects with clear requirements
    
* Agile Model: 
  Work is divided into small cycles called sprints
  Continuous feedback and improvement
  Advantages: Flexible , Fast delivery , Customer involvement
  Disadvantages: Requires experienced team , Less documentation
  Use case: Dynamic projects, startups
    
* Spiral Model: 
  Combination of Waterfall + Risk Analysis
  Development happens in loops (spirals)
  Advantages: Good risk management , Suitable for large projects
  Disadvantages: Complex , Costly
  Use case: Large and high-risk projects4️
    
* V-Model (Verification & Validation):
  Each development phase has a corresponding testing phase
  Advantages: Early testing , High quality
  Disadvantages: Rigid , Not flexible
  Use case: Safety-critical systems

* Incremental Model:
  Software is developed in small increments
  Each increment adds features
  Advantages: Early delivery , Easy testing
  Disadvantages: Needs good planning
  Use case: Modular system

* Iterative Model:
  Repeated development cycles
  Improve software with each iteration
  Advantages: Early working model , Flexible
  Disadvantages: Needs strong design
  Use case: Large applications

* Prototype Model: 
  Build a working prototype first
  Customer feedback is taken
  Advantages: Better understanding of requirements , Reduced risk
  Disadvantages: Extra cost , Poor design risk
  Use case: When requirements are unclear
